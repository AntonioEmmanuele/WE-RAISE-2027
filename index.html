Call for Papers

Edge computing is reshaping intelligent and interconnected systems by moving computation and decision-making closer to where data is generated. This approach enables low-latency services, reduces communication overhead, and supports local decision-making in applications ranging from industrial automation and smart infrastructures to autonomous devices.The growing adoption of machine learning at the edge extends these capabilities through local inference, collaborative training, and adaptation to changing operating conditions.

However, deploying these functionalities on embedded platforms introduces challenges related to limited computing resources, memory capacity, energy budgets, and communication bandwidth.

The distributed and often physically accessible nature of edge devices also raises security, privacy, and reliability concerns. Protecting sensitive information, establishing trustworthy device identities, securing communications, and maintaining the integrity of learning processes require mechanisms tailored to constrained and heterogeneous platforms. Meanwhile, hardware faults, environmental disturbances, and malicious attacks can compromise inference and system operation.

These challenges call for design methodologies that jointly consider computational efficiency, dependable execution, and security across hardware and software layers.

WE-RAISE 2027 aims to bring together researchers and practitioners from academia and industry working on edge computing, embedded machine learning, computer architectures, hardware security, and distributed systems. The workshop will explore techniques and architectural solutions for efficient, reliable, and secure edge intelligence, focusing on the interplay between learning algorithms, computing platforms, communication infrastructures, and hardware-rooted trust.

Topics of interest include on-device inference, model optimisation and compression, federated and collaborative learning, distributed computation, adaptive learning, and explainable edge AI. Hardware/software co-design, specialised accelerators, and fault-tolerant inference will be addressed alongside lightweight cryptographic protocols, Physical Unclonable Functions, trusted execution environments, secure provisioning, and remote attestation. The scope also encompasses privacy-preserving learning and protection against side-channel attacks, fault injection, adversarial examples, model poisoning, and model extraction.

Particular emphasiswill be placed on research at the intersection of these areas, including the integration of hardware-rooted security into distributed learning and the joint optimisation of predictive performance and interpretability alongside computational efficiency, reliability and security.

Topics of interest

We welcome research contributions on the following topics, including work at their intersection:

  • On-device machine learning inference and training for resource-constrained edge devices.
  • Model optimisation and compression under compute, memory, energy and bandwidth constraints.
  • Federated and collaborative learning, distributed computation and adaptive learning at the edge.
  • Explainable edge AI and joint optimisation of predictive performance and interpretability.
  • Hardware/software co-design and specialised accelerators for edge intelligence.
  • Reliable deployment, dependable execution and fault-tolerant inference.
  • Lightweight cryptographic protocols, secure communications and device authentication.
  • Physical Unclonable Functions, trusted execution environments and hardware-rooted trust.
  • Secure provisioning, remote attestation and trustworthy device identities.
  • Privacy-preserving learning and hardware-rooted security for distributed learning.
  • Protection against side-channel attacks, fault injection and adversarial examples.
  • Defences against model poisoning and model extraction, and integrity of learning processes.

Most valuable contributions will be invited to extend their work on a dedicated special-issue.

Submission information

The planned format includes reviewed papers, an invited talk and a keynote. Papers will undergo double-blind review with at least three independent reviews per paper.
